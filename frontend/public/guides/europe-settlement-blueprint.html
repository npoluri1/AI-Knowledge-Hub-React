09 Health Insurance — Single, Spouse, Children

Health is the line item that moves most between countries — and it moves violently. In Germany, Norway, Iceland and the Netherlands, your contribution covers your spouse and children at no additional premium. In Switzerland and Ireland, family cover is charged per person and can become the most expensive line item in the household.

Where your family is free

  • Germany (statutory GKV): spouse and all children covered from your contribution. Zero extra. Dental, mental health, and hospital are inside the same package.
  • Norway (Folketrygden): spouse and children under 18 covered automatically, full cost, with an exemption ceiling for your â‚¬83k income.
  • Iceland: state health insurance covers the entire household, including the spouse.
  • Netherlands: every adult carries their own zorgverzekering (~â‚¬145â€“190/mo, income-tested with a zorgtoeslag refund) and children under 18 are free.
  • Denmark, Sweden, Finland, France, Austria: the state covers the family, mostly through employer or municipal funding rather than a personal premium.

Where family cover costs extra

  • Switzerland: mandatory private insurance, roughly CHF 380â€“700 per adult per month, with no automatic child cover â€” a family of four can pay CHF 1,500+/month. Buy a family tariff and cap the child's premium yourself.
  • Ireland: the public system is free but has real waiting lists for consultants. Private cover is common; children's HIC (8%) is free, and adult treatment in a public hospital is free. Employer plans vary widely.
  • Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg: compulsory supplementary health insurance (mutualitÃ© / aanvullende verzekering) is a small family fee, roughly â‚¬40â€“110 per adult per month, cheaper if you are healthy and group-cover is offered by your employer.
  • Czechia and Poland: the public statutory fund is compulsory and cheap; the real cost is a private clinic plan if you want shorter waiting times and English consultations.
Two things to do before you sign anything: (1) Ask the employer explicitly â€” "is the health contribution the same for my spouse, and are children added to my policy at no extra premium?" The answer changes the net comparison by hundreds of euros a month. (2) Check the deductible (Selbstbehalt in Germany, eigen risico in the Netherlands). A low premium with a â‚¬1,500+ annual deductible is a bad deal for a family that expects to use healthcare.
