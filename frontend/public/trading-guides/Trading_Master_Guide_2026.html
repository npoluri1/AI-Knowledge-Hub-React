📖 SECTION 2: TRADING GLOSSARY — Every Word You Need to Know

Below is a complete dictionary of words used daily in trading. These are the terms you will see on Bloomberg, CNBC, broker apps, and financial news every single day.

💱 Basic Trading Terms

WORDMEANING (Simple English)EXAMPLE
STOCK / SHAREA small piece of ownership in a company. When you buy 1 share of Apple, you own a tiny part of Apple."I bought 10 shares of Apple at $325 each = $3,250 invested."
BULLMarket is going UP. Prices are rising. Optimism. Named after how a bull attacks — horns thrust upward."This is a bull market — S&P 500 is up 20% this year."
BEARMarket is going DOWN. Prices are falling. Fear. Named after how a bear attacks — paws swipe downward."We are in a bear market — stocks have fallen 25% from highs."
BULL MARKETA prolonged period where stock prices rise 20%+ from recent lows."The US has been in a bull market since 2023."
BEAR MARKETA period where stock prices fall 20%+ from recent highs."Crypto entered a bear market after hitting $100K."
RECESSIONEconomic activity shrinks for 2+ consecutive quarters (6 months). GDP falls. Jobs are lost."If oil keeps rising, a recession is possible in Q4."
CRASHSudden, sharp drop in prices (10%+ in a short time). Panic selling."The market crashed 8% in one day during COVID."
CORRECTIONStock market falls 10% from its recent high. Normal and healthy. Happens ~1-2 times per year."S&P 500 entered correction after falling from 8,000 to 7,200."
RALLYA strong upward move in prices over days or weeks."Stocks rallied 5% after the Fed held rates steady."
PULLBACKA small decline (3-10%) from recent highs. Not as severe as a correction."Expect a pullback before the next leg up."
VOLATILITYHow much prices move up and down. High volatility = big swings. Low volatility = calm markets."VIX at 30 means high volatility — big daily moves expected."
LiquidityHow easily you can buy or sell an asset without affecting its price. Cash is the most liquid."Apple has high liquidity — millions of shares trade daily."
Market CapTotal value of a company = Share Price x Number of Shares. Shows company size."NVIDIA market cap is $5.2 trillion — world's 3rd largest company."
VOLUMENumber of shares traded in a day. High volume = lots of interest. Low volume = quiet."NVDA traded 130M shares today — very high volume."

💵 Order Types (How to Buy/Sell)

ORDER TYPEMEANINGWHEN TO USE
MARKET ORDERBuy/sell IMMEDIATELY at the current market price. Fast but you might get a slightly different price than expected.When you want to enter/exit NOW and don't care about small price differences.
LIMIT ORDERBuy/sell at a SPECIFIC price you set. Order only executes when price reaches your target.When you want to buy at a specific price: "Buy NVDA at $210" (not at current $217).
STOP LOSS ORDERAutomatically sell if price drops to a certain level. Protects you from big losses.Buy NVDA at $217, set stop loss at $195. If NVDA falls to $195, auto-sell to limit loss.
STOP LIMIT ORDERA combination — trigger a limit order when price hits your stop level.More control than stop loss. Avoids selling at bad prices during flash crashes.
TRAILING STOPStop loss that moves UP as price moves up. Locks in profits automatically.Buy at $200, trailing stop $20. If stock goes to $250, stop moves to $230. If it drops to $230, auto-sell.
FRACTIONAL ORDERBuy a FRACTION of a share (not whole). E.g., 0.5 shares of Berkshire at $700K = $350K.When share price is too high to buy whole shares. Moomoo, Syfe support this.

🚀 SECTION 3: HOW TRADING ACTUALLY WORKS — Step by Step

The Complete Journey from Zero to Trading

STEP 1: Open a Brokerage Account

A brokerage account is like a bank account, but for buying stocks instead of saving money. You deposit cash, then use it to buy investments.

👍 EXAMPLE

You open a Moomoo account → Verify with Singpass (3 minutes) → Transfer SGD 1,000 via FAST → Now you have SGD 1,000 to invest.

Best brokers for beginners in Singapore:

  • Moomoo SG — Easiest app, zero US commission, SG stores
  • Syfe Brokerage — SGD 1 minimum, simplest onboarding
  • Webull SG — Zero commission on everything
  • Tiger Brokers — Best for CPF/SRS investing
  • Interactive Brokers (IBKR) — Cheapest for global ETFs

STEP 2: Deposit Money (Fund Account)

Transfer money from your bank account to your brokerage account. This is like loading money onto a card before you shop.

👍 EXAMPLE

Log into DBS/OCBC/UOB → Transfer to Moomoo's DBS bank account → Use FAST (instant) or PayNow → Money appears in Moomoo in minutes.

Minimum amounts:

  • Syfe: SGD 1
  • Moomoo: SGD 100+
  • Tiger: SGD 100+
  • IBKR: SGD 0 (no minimum)

STEP 3: Choose What to Buy

You can buy individual stocks (risky, needs research) or ETFs (diversified, safer for beginners).

👍 EXAMPLE

Option A (Safer): Buy CSPX ETF — instantly own 500 US companies (Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, etc.) in one purchase.
Option B (Riskier): Buy only Nvidia stock — all your money in one company.

💡 BEGINNER TIP: Start with ETFs. They spread your risk across hundreds of companies. Individual stocks are for when you understand a company well.

STEP 4: Place Your Order

Search for the stock/ETF ticker, choose quantity, select order type, and click BUY.

👍 EXAMPLE

Open Moomoo → Search "CSPX" → Click BUY → Select "Limit Order" at $95 → Quantity: 10 shares → Total cost: $950 → Confirm → Done!

⚠ ALWAYS use Limit Orders, not Market Orders. Market orders can execute at bad prices during volatile times.

STEP 5: Monitor & Manage Your Portfolio

Check your portfolio regularly (weekly, not every minute). Rebalance quarterly. Sell when your thesis changes, not when you feel scared.

GOOD HABIT

Check portfolio every Friday evening. Read 1-2 news articles. Note what's up and why.

BAD HABIT

Checking every 5 minutes. Panic selling on red days. FOMO buying on green days.

PRO HABIT

Maintain a trading journal. Write down WHY you bought and WHEN you plan to sell.

🏢 SECTION 4: STOCKS — Complete Guide

What is a Stock?

A stock (also called a share or equity) represents partial ownership of a company. When Apple has 15 billion shares and you own 10, you own 10/15,000,000,000 = 0.000000067% of Apple. If Apple makes $100 billion profit, your 10 shares get 0.000000067% of that = $0.0067 in earnings.

📡 SECTION 5: ETFs — The Best Way to Start Investing

What is an ETF?

An ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) is a basket of stocks/bonds that you can buy as a single unit, just like buying one share. It gives you instant diversification — instead of picking one stock, you own hundreds at once.

👍 SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Instead of buying Apple ($325) + Microsoft ($490) + Nvidia ($217) + Amazon ($266) + Google ($347) separately = $1,645 total, you buy 1 share of CSPX ETF for ~$95 and you instantly OWN all 500 companies in the S&P 500. That's the power of ETFs.

🏦 SECTION 7: BONDS — Fixed Income Guide

What is a Bond?

A bond is like lending money to a government or company. They pay you interest (called yield) and return your money at a set date (maturity). Bonds are generally safer than stocks but offer lower returns.

👍 EXAMPLE

You buy a US 10-Year Treasury Bond for $1,000 at 4.68% yield.
→ You receive $46.80 per year in interest (paid every 6 months = $23.40 each).
→ After 10 years, you get your $1,000 back.
→ Total earnings: $468 in interest over 10 years.

ETF vs Mutual Fund:

🎯 SECTION 11: TRADING STRATEGIES — From Beginner to Pro

STRATEGY 1: Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA) — BEST FOR BEGINNERS

Invest a fixed amount at regular intervals (e.g., every month), regardless of whether the market is up or down.

👍 EXAMPLE

Every 1st of the month, invest SGD 500 into CSPX ETF:
• Sep: Buy at $95 → get 5.26 shares
• Oct: Buy at $88 (market dipped!) → get 5.68 shares
• Nov: Buy at $92 → get 5.43 shares
• Dec: Buy at $97 → get 5.15 shares

Total: SGD 2,000 invested, 21.52 shares, average cost = $92.93 per share. You bought MORE shares when prices were LOW!

Why it works: Removes emotion. No need to time the market. Historically outperforms trying to pick the "perfect" entry point.

Best for: ETFs (CSPX, VWRA, ES3). Works for any market condition.

STRATEGY 2: Buy the Dip (BTDF)

Wait for the market to drop 5-10%, then buy quality stocks at a discount.

👍 EXAMPLE

NVDA is at $236 (52-week high). You set alerts:
• Buy 1st tranche at $215 (10% drop)
• Buy 2nd tranche at $200 (15% drop)
• Buy 3rd tranche at $185 (22% drop)

📁 SECTION 14: OPTIONS TRADING — A Step-by-Step Tutorial

⚠ HIGH RISK WARNING: Options are complex and you can lose MORE than you invest. Only trade options after you fully understand them. Start on a paper account. This section is educational only, NOT financial advice.

📝 A. OPTIONS BASICS — THE FIVE THINGS YOU MUST KNOW

CONCEPTPLAIN ENGLISHEXAMPLE
Call OptionGives you the RIGHT to BUY a stock at a set price before a date.Buy a CALL on NVDA at $220 (strike) — you bet the price will go UP.
Put OptionGives you the RIGHT to SELL a stock at a set price before a date.Buy a PUT on NVDA at $200 — you bet the price will go DOWN.
Strike PriceThe price at which you can buy/sell the stock.$220 in the example above.
Expiration DateThe last day the option is valid.3rd Friday of the month (standard).
PremiumThe price you PAY to buy an option (cost).$5 per share x 100 shares = $500 per contract.

💡 CRITICAL RULE: OPTIONS COME IN BLOCKS OF 100 SHARES

1 option contract = 100 shares of the underlying stock.

👍 EXAMPLE

NVDA premium is $5 per share for a call option.
One contract costs: $5 × 100 shares = $500 premium.
If NVDA rises and your option is worth $8 per share: you can sell it for $8 × 100 = $800. Profit = $300 (minus fees).

🏆 B. FOUR MUST-KNOW OPTIONS STRATEGIES

STRATEGY 1: Covered Call (Income) — LOW MEDIUM RISK

What: You OWN the stock, and you SELL a call option on it.

👍 EXAMPLE

You own 100 shares of DBS (cost S$46 each).
You SELL a $50 call option, collecting $2 premium × 100 = S$200 income.
• If DBS stays under $50: You keep $200 + your shares.
• If DBS goes above $50: Your shares are sold at $50. Profit = ($50-$46) x 100 = $400 + $200 premium.

Risk: Miss out on gains above the strike price. Best for: Generating monthly income on stocks you own.

STRATEGY 2: Cash-Secured Put (Buying on Discount) — MEDIUM RISK

What: You SELL a put option, getting paid to agree to buy a stock at a discount.

👍 EXAMPLE

NVDA is $217. You want to buy at $200.
You SELL a $200 put, collecting $4 premium × 100 = $400 income.
• If NVDA stays above $200: You keep $400, no obligation.
• If NVDA drops below $200: You buy 100 shares at $200 (which you wanted!) and keep the $400.

Risk: Stock could keep falling, forcing you to buy at $200 when it's at $180. Requires: Cash to cover the purchase.

STRATEGY 3: Long Call (Speculation) — HIGH RISK

What: You BUY a call, betting the stock will rise before expiration.

👍 EXAMPLE

You buy 1 NVDA $220 call for $5 premium ($500 total).
• NVDA rises to $250 before expiry: Your call is now worth $30 × 100 = $3,000. Profit = $2,500 (500%!).
• NVDA stays below $220: Your option expires worthless. You LOSE the full $500.

Risk: You can lose 100% of the premium. Time decay works AGAINST you (options lose value each day).

STRATEGY 4: Protective Put (Insurance) — LOW RISK

What: You OWN a stock and BUY a put to insure against a crash.

👍 EXAMPLE

You own 100 shares of AAPL at $210.
You BUY a $190 put for $3 × 100 = $300 insurance.
• If AAPL crashes to $150: Your put lets you sell at $190. Loss capped at $20/share instead of $60.
• If AAPL rises: Put expires worthless. You only lost the $300 premium (premium = cost of peace of mind).

⚠ C. OPTIONS RISKS & PITFALLS

PITFALLWHAT HAPPENSHOW TO AVOID
Time DecayOptions lose value every single day as expiration nears.Buy options with 30+ days left, avoid day-of-expiry.
Buying too latePremium drops fast in final weeks.Take profit early, don't wait for the last day.
100% LossOptions can expire worthless — you lose everything paid.Only use money you can afford to lose entirely.
Naked OptionsSelling options without owning the stock can lead to huge losses.Never sell naked calls/puts as a beginner.
OvertradingFees and spreads eat into small profits.Trade few, high-conviction setups.

💰 A SIMPLE RULE OF THUMB

As a beginner, limit options to 1-2% of your total portfolio. Use covered calls and cash-secured puts only. Avoid buying naked calls/puts with more than a few hundred dollars.

💡 FOUR QUESTIONS BEFORE EVERY OPTIONS TRADE

• Can I afford to lose 100% of this premium?
• Do I understand what makes it go up or down?
• Have I checked the expiration and strike?
• Would I be fine if it expires worthless?

📉 SECTION 15: TECHNICAL ANALYSIS & CHART READING

💡 WHAT IS TECHNICAL ANALYSIS?

Technical analysis studies price charts and volume to predict future price movement. Unlike fundamental analysis (studying company financials), technical analysis assumes all information is already reflected in the price. It's about reading market psychology through charts.

📎 A. THE THREE BASIC CHART TYPES

LINE CHART

Connects closing prices with a line. Simplest view of price trend over time.

Use for: Long-term trend overview (months/years).

BAR CHART

Each bar shows open, high, low, and close for a period. More detail than a line chart.

Use for: Daily/weekly analysis of price range.

CANDLESTICK CHART (MOST POPULAR)

Each "candle" shows the open, high, low, and close. Green/white candle = price went UP. Red/black candle = price went DOWN.

Use for: Most traders. Shows market emotion clearly.

HOW TO READ A CANDLE

Body = range between open and close.
Wicks (shadows) = the highest and lowest prices touched.
Long lower wick = buyers stepped in (bullish signal).
Long upper wick = sellers pushed price down (bearish signal).

📏 B. THE FOUR ESSENTIAL INDICATORS

1. MOVING AVERAGES (MA) — Trend Direction

What: Averages of past prices, smoothed into a line. Two key ones:

• 50-day MA (short-term): If price is ABOVE it, short-term trend is up.
• 200-day MA (long-term): If price is ABOVE it, long-term trend is up.

👍 GOLDEN CROSS vs DEATH CROSS

Golden Cross: 50-day MA crosses ABOVE the 200-day MA → BULLISH signal.
Death Cross: 50-day MA crosses BELOW the 200-day MA → BEARISH signal.

2. RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — Overbought/Oversold

Measures momentum on a scale of 0 to 100.

• RSI above 70 = OVERBOUGHT. Price may pull back (near a top).
• RSI below 30 = OVERSOLD. Price may bounce (near a bottom).
• RSI around 50 = Neutral, no clear signal.

👍 USE IT

NVDA RSI hits 75 → consider taking some profit or waiting for a pullback before buying. RSI hits 28 → watch for a bounce, a possible dip-buy entry.

3. SUPPORT & RESISTANCE — Price Floors and Ceilings

• Support: A price level where buyers repeatedly step in (a "floor").
• Resistance: A price level where sellers repeatedly step in (a "ceiling").

👍 EXAMPLE

NVDA keeps bouncing off $200 (support) and stalling at $240 (resistance).
• Break ABOVE $240 → could run higher (new upward move).
• Break BELOW $200 → warns of a deeper drop.

4. VOLUME — Confirming Moves

Volume = how many shares traded. It confirms whether a price move is "real".

• Price rises WITH high volume → strong, genuine bullish move.
• Price rises WITH low volume → weak move, may not last.
• Price drops WITH high volume → strong selling, be cautious.

🎯 C. A SIMPLE BEGINNER CHART-READING CHECKLIST

STEPWHAT TO CHECK
1Is the price ABOVE or BELOW the 200-day moving average? (Trend up or down?)
2Is the 50-day MA above or below the 200-day MA? (Golden cross or death cross?)
3What is the RSI? (Over 70 = hot, under 30 = cold.)
4Where is the nearest support and resistance? (Set your entry and stop-loss there.)
5Is today's move on high or low volume? (Real or fake move?)
6Combine 2+ signals before acting. NEVER rely on just ONE indicator.

Remember: Technical analysis is a probability tool, not a crystal ball. It improves odds; it does not guarantee outcomes.
