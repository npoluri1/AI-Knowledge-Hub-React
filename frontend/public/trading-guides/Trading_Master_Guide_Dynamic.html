Trading Glossary

SEARCHABLE
🔍
Stock
A share of ownership in a company. Buy a stock and you own a tiny piece of the business.
ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)
A basket of assets (stocks, bonds, gold) you buy like a single stock. One purchase gives instant diversification.
Bull Market
A period when prices are rising overall. Optimism dominates.
Bear Market
A period when prices fall ~20%+ from highs. Pessimism dominates.
Dividend
A share of a company's profit paid to shareholders, usually quarterly. A form of passive income.
Bull/Bear
Bull = buyer/optimistic. Bear = seller/pessimistic.
Stop-Loss
A preset price at which you automatically sell to cap your loss. Discipline that saves accounts.
DCA (Dollar-Cost Averaging)
Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, ignoring price. Smooths out volatility over time.
Index
A benchmark tracking a group of stocks, e.g. S&P 500, STI, Nifty 50.
Benchmark
The standard/index an investment is compared against (e.g. does your fund beat the S&P 500?).
Liquidity
How easily you can buy/sell without moving the price. Cash is liquid; property is not.
Volatility
How much and how fast a price swings. High volatility = big moves, both up and down.
Market Cap
Total value of a company = share price x shares outstanding. Large-cap > $10B, small-cap < $2B.
Portfolio
Your full collection of investments across stocks, bonds, ETFs, gold, cash, etc.
Rebalancing
Realigning your portfolio back to target percentages periodically to control risk.
Yield
Annual income as a % of price. Dividend yield = annual dividend / price.
Blue Chip
A large, established, financially strong company (e.g. DBS, Apple, HDFC Bank).
IPO (Initial Public Offering)
The first time a private company sells shares to the public.
Margin
Borrowing money from your broker to trade. Amplifies gains AND losses. Dangerous for beginners.
Leverage
Using borrowed funds/derivatives to amplify exposure. Can multiply losses to zero.
Short Selling
Borrowing shares to sell now, hoping to buy back cheaper later. Betting on a price FALL.
Call Option
Right (not obligation) to BUY a stock at a set price before expiry. Bullish tool.
Put Option
Right (not obligation) to SELL a stock at a set price before expiry. Bearish tool / insurance.
Strike Price
The fixed price at which an option lets you buy (call) or sell (put) the underlying.
Premium (Options)
The price/cost you pay to buy an option, or collect when selling one.
REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust)
A company owning income-producing property. Distributes most profit as dividends.
Bond
A loan you give to a government/company that pays interest and returns principal at maturity.
Interest Rate
The cost of borrowing money. Central banks raise/lower it to control inflation and growth.
Inflation
The rate at which prices of goods/services rise, eroding purchasing power.
Diversification
Spreading money across assets/sectors/countries to reduce risk. Don't put all eggs in one basket.
Technical Analysis
Predicting price moves from charts, volume and indicators (RSI, moving averages, support).
Fundamental Analysis
Valuing a company from financials like earnings, revenue, debt and growth.
RSI
Relative Strength Index. 0-100 momentum gauge. Above 70 = overbought, below 30 = oversold.
Moving Average
Smoothed average price over N days. 50-day (short) & 200-day (long) reveal trend.
Support
A price level where buyers repeatedly step in — a "floor".
Resistance
A price level where sellers repeatedly step in — a "ceiling".
Volume
Number of shares traded. Confirms whether a price move is strong or weak.
Risk:Reward Ratio
Compares potential loss to potential gain. Minimum 2:1 means risking 1 to make 2.
Position Sizing
How much of your portfolio each trade uses. Max 5% per stock is a common safety rule.
Paper Trading
Practicing trades with fake money in real market conditions. Zero risk, great for learning.
Trading Journal
A log of every trade: why you bought, stop-loss, target, result and lesson.
Assets Under Management (AUM)
Total market value of assets a fund/broker manages for clients.
Net Asset Value (NAV)
The per-share value of a fund's holdings, used to price mutual funds.
Expense Ratio
The annual fee a fund charges, as a % of your investment. Lower is better.

How Trading Actually Works

5 STEPS
💡
You don't need to be a genius. You need a system. Here is the 5-step path every trader follows.

① Open a Broker Account

Register with a licensed broker (Moomoo, Tiger, IBKR, etc.). Complete identity verification with Singpass. Fund your account with a bank transfer.

② Do Your Research

Decide WHAT to buy and WHY. Use fundamental analysis (is the company good?) and technical analysis (is the price trending?). Never buy on a tip alone.

③ Place an Order

Choose order type: Market (buy now) or Limit (buy only at/below a set price). Set your position size and a stop-loss before clicking buy.

④ Monitor & Manage

Check your positions weekly, not daily. Respect your stop-loss. Take profit on your targets. Write every trade in your journal.

⑤ Review & Improve

Monthly review: what worked, what didn't, what to change. Continuous improvement is what separates winners from gamblers.

⚠ Golden Rules

1) Never invest money you need soon. 2) Never skip a stop-loss. 3) Expect losses — profit is what's left after a string of small losses.

Bonds & Fixed Income

10Y = 4.68%

🏥 What is a Bond?

You lend money to a government/company. They pay you interest and return your principal at maturity. Lower risk than stocks.

💲 Treasury Interest

The 10Y US Treasury is ~4.68%. When rates fall, existing bond prices rise. Bonds are a ballast against stock crashes.

📬 Easy Bond Picks

• SGOV (US short-term treasuries)
• Singapore Savings Bonds (SSB)
• T-bills (safe, liquid, good for short term)

Mutual Funds & Index Funds

PASSIVE INCOME

🏷 Mutual Fund

Actively managed pool of money. A manager picks stocks to beat the market. Higher fees (expense ratio).

📊 Index Fund

Passively tracks an index (S&P 500, Nifty). Lower fees, easy to own, historically strong.

⚠ Watch the Fees

Fund fee of 2% vs 0.2% over 20 years can equal tens of thousands in lost growth. Fees matter a lot.

Technical Analysis & Chart Reading

PATTERNS

📈 Moving Averages

50-day (short) & 200-day (long). Price above = uptrend. Golden cross (50 over 200) = bullish; death cross = bearish.

🎯 RSI (0-100)

Above 70 = overbought (may pull back). Below 30 = oversold (may bounce). ~50 = neutral.

🏭 Support & Resistance

Support = "floor" where buyers step in. Resistance = "ceiling" where sellers step in. Breakouts above resistance can run.

🔖 Volume

Confirms moves. Price up + high volume = strong. Price up + low volume = weak / likely to reverse.

💡
Never rely on ONE indicator. Combine trend (MA) + momentum (RSI) + levels (support/resistance) + volume for higher confidence.

Options Trading — Step by Step

ADVANCED
⚠
HIGH RISK. You can lose more than you invest. Only trade options after full understanding and on a paper account first.

📖 Call Option

Right to BUY at a set price by expiry. Bullish bet. Example: buy a NVDA $220 call.

📖 Put Option

Right to SELL at a set price by expiry. Bearish bet / insurance.

📅 Expiry & Strike

Expiry = last valid day (3rd Friday). Strike = fixed price. 1 contract = 100 shares.

💲 Covered Call

Own 100 shares + sell a call = collect premium income. Low-medium risk. Great for DBS/NVDA holders.

🏖 Cash-Secured Put

Sell a put, get paid to be ready to buy at a discount. Requires cash to cover.

🧮 Long Call

Buy a call, bet on a big rise. High risk: can lose 100% of premium via time decay.

⚠
Beginner rule: limit options to 1-2% of your portfolio. Use covered calls and cash-secured puts. Avoid naked options entirely.
